    Planning case management

    Prototype

    This is an illustrative concept based on initial desk research.

    It has not been validated with practitioners. Use it to explore ideas and provide feedback, not as authoritative guidance.

    Planning case management supports the receipt, assessment and determination of planning applications and related cases.

    It does not operate independently. It exchanges information with other planning capabilities and with common council capabilities such as document management, payments, identity and access management, notifications, geospatial information and records management.

    This page helps councils:

    • understand the relationships between planning case management and other capabilities;
    • identify integration and data requirements;
    • distinguish planning-specific functionality from common capabilities;
    • consider opportunities to reuse existing council services; and
    • assess the architectural implications of changing or replacing a planning case-management system.

    Status: Illustrative pattern
    Evidence maturity: Partially validated
    Use this page for: Early discovery, options assessment, procurement preparation and solution design
    Do not use it as: A specification for an individual council or procurement

    Who this is for

    This guidance is primarily intended for people responsible for technology decisions affecting planning services. The descriptions below reflect roles as they commonly appear in local government, though titles and responsibilities vary between councils.

    Technical architects and solution designers

    People who design how systems connect, define integration patterns, and make decisions about capability boundaries. They need to understand what planning case management depends on and what information flows between components, so they can design solutions that work within the council's wider architecture.

    Use this page to: identify integration points, understand information exchange requirements, and assess where shared capabilities can serve planning needs.

    Digital and IT leaders

    Heads of digital, chief technology officers, or service directors who make investment decisions about council technology. They need to understand whether replacing a planning system is a like-for-like change or a wider architectural decision affecting shared services.

    Use this page to: understand the scope and dependencies of planning technology, challenge assumptions about what must be bundled, and inform business cases.

    Procurement professionals

    People who write requirements, evaluate supplier responses, and manage technology contracts. They need to avoid inadvertently specifying and paying for functionality that already exists elsewhere in the council.

    Use this page to: distinguish planning-specific from common requirements, and ask informed questions about integration, portability and exit.

    Planning service managers

    Heads of planning or development management who own the service but may not be responsible for the technology that supports it. They need to articulate what their service depends on without needing to be technical.

    Use this page to: understand what your system relies on beyond itself, and have informed conversations with IT colleagues about change.

    Capability map

    Planning case management is the central capability considered on this page. It commonly interacts with the following capabilities:

    Document management

    Stores application forms, plans, supporting evidence, correspondence, consultation responses and decision notices.

    Payments

    Takes and records application fees and other chargeable payments.

    Identity and access management

    Provides appropriate access for applicants, agents, officers, consultees and members of the public.

    Notifications

    Issues acknowledgements, consultation notices, requests for information, status updates and decisions.

    Geospatial information

    Connects an application to a site, boundary, constraint, designation or other location-based information.

    Records management

    Applies retention, disposal, audit and accessibility requirements to the formal case record.

    These capabilities describe what the council needs to be able to do. They do not prescribe whether each capability should be provided by a single planning product, an existing corporate service, or a combination of systems.

    How the capabilities relate

    A planning application may pass through the following capability relationships:

    1. An applicant or agent provides application information and supporting documents.

    2. Identity and access management controls access to online services and restricted case information.

    3. Payments processes any required fee and returns payment status.

    4. Geospatial information associates the application with the relevant site and location-based constraints.

    5. Document management stores plans, evidence, correspondence and consultation responses.

    6. Planning case management brings the relevant information together to support validation, assessment and determination.

    7. Notifications communicates progress, consultation activity, information requests and the final decision.

    8. Records management ensures that the authoritative case record remains available and is managed appropriately.

    Information exchanged

    Information exchanged between planning case management and related capabilities
    From To Example information
    Applicant-facing service Planning case management Application details, applicant information and site reference
    Payments Planning case management Payment reference, amount and payment status
    Geospatial information Planning case management Site boundary, address, constraints and designations
    Document management Planning case management Plans, reports, photographs and supporting evidence
    Planning case management Notifications Recipient, message type, case status and notification trigger
    Consultees Planning case management Consultation comments, representations and supporting documents
    Planning case management Records management Decision, supporting evidence, audit history and final case record

    The precise information exchanged, its format and the system of record should be established locally.

    Evidence and limitations

    Current level of confidence

    Statement Confidence Basis and limitation
    Planning case management has relationships with documents, payments, identity, notifications, location information and records. To validate Proposed model from desk research. Should be tested with planning and architecture practitioners.
    Councils may benefit from reusing established corporate capabilities. To validate Suggested architectural pattern, not a universally applicable rule. Value depends on local capability maturity.
    Clear ownership and interfaces are needed between related capabilities. Provisional Follows from the proposed separation of capabilities but requires validation against real architectures.
    LGAM could support planning-system replacement and procurement decisions. To validate with users Research should establish which practitioners make these decisions and whether this guidance would materially help.

    Known limitations

    This page does not yet:

    • define a complete planning capability model;
    • specify mandatory technical interfaces or data standards;
    • establish which system should be authoritative in every council;
    • assess the maturity of existing council capabilities;
    • replace service-specific discovery or architecture work; or
    • recommend a particular supplier, product or deployment model.
