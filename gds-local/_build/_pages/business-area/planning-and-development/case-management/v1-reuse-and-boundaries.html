    Reuse and boundaries

    Prototype

    This is an illustrative concept based on initial desk research.

    It has not been validated with practitioners. Use it to explore ideas and provide feedback, not as authoritative guidance.

    Deciding what belongs inside a planning case-management product and what should be provided by shared council capabilities.

    When a council procures or replaces a planning system, it often inherits a package of capabilities that may overlap with existing corporate services. This page provides questions and a suggested pattern to help councils make deliberate decisions about those boundaries.

    This page is part of the Planning case management concept. Read the overview first for context on the capabilities and relationships described here.

    Questions about reuse of council capabilities

    These questions help establish whether existing shared capabilities could serve planning needs, or whether a planning-specific version is justified.

    Existing provision

    • Does the council already provide a corporate payment, identity, notification or document-management capability?
    • Is that capability currently used by other council services?
    • What is its current maturity and capacity?

    Suitability for planning

    • Can the planning service use that capability without creating unacceptable operational or user-experience constraints?
    • Does it support the volume, complexity and statutory requirements of planning?
    • Would adaptation be disproportionately costly compared to a separate solution?

    Duplication risk

    • Would including the same capability within a planning product duplicate an existing service?
    • Would duplication create inconsistency in citizen experience or staff processes?
    • Would it create parallel maintenance, licensing or support costs?

    Justification for specialisation

    • Is there a clear reason why planning requires a specialist version of this capability?
    • Is that reason based on genuine service needs or primarily on the planning product's architecture?
    • Would the specialist version need to integrate with the corporate version anyway?

    Questions about the scope of the planning product

    These questions help examine where the boundary of a planning case-management product should sit.

    • Is the functionality genuinely specific to planning?
    • Is it a common council capability being bundled into a planning product?
    • Would separating the capability improve reuse, flexibility or consistency?
    • Would separation introduce disproportionate cost, complexity or operational risk?
    • Is the proposed boundary based on user and service needs, or primarily on the current supplier's product design?

    These questions are not intended to produce a single correct answer. They are intended to surface assumptions and make the boundary decision a conscious one rather than a default inherited from the product.

    Suggested architectural pattern

    Reuse shared council capabilities where appropriate

    Where suitable shared capabilities already exist, the planning case-management service should integrate with them rather than automatically procuring separate equivalents.

    For example:

    • use the council's shared payment capability to process planning fees;
    • use the council's identity and access capability where authenticated access is required;
    • use shared notification services for email or message delivery;
    • use corporate document or records-management capabilities where they meet planning requirements; and
    • use agreed geospatial services and identifiers to reference sites and locations consistently.

    The planning case-management capability should retain the information needed to manage the case, including references to relevant payments, documents, people, locations, consultations and decisions.

    Why consider this pattern?

    Potential advantages include:

    • reducing duplication of common functionality;
    • providing a more consistent experience across council services;
    • making common capabilities easier to manage and improve;
    • reducing dependence on a single planning-system supplier; and
    • allowing components to change without necessarily replacing the complete service.

    When this pattern may not be suitable

    Reuse should not be treated as an absolute rule. A shared capability may not meet planning needs where it cannot support:

    • the required information or workflow;
    • planning-specific access controls;
    • statutory or records-management requirements;
    • necessary service levels or operational processes; or
    • reliable integration with the planning service.

    The decision should be based on the council's context and evidence, rather than an assumption that either an integrated suite or separate shared capabilities will always be preferable.

    Recording decisions

    When applying this guidance, record:

    • the capabilities included in scope;
    • the proposed boundary of the planning case-management product;
    • existing council capabilities considered for reuse;
    • options considered and reasons for the preferred approach;
    • assumptions that still require validation; and
    • any departure from the suggested pattern.

    Recording these decisions explicitly helps the council revisit them when circumstances change, such as when a shared capability matures, a contract expires, or a new requirement emerges.
